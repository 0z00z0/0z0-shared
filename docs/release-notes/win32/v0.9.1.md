**The arithmetic that fits a window into a work area.** One new type; nothing else moves.

**Released** 2026-09-24

## Added

- **`WindowFit`** — window placement as plain numbers, so a caller on any user-interface framework
  measures and this places.
  - `Fit(desired, requiredHeight, workArea)` — grown where the content needs more height, cut down
    to the work area, and moved inside it. A rectangle sharing no pixel with the work area is
    re-centred rather than clamped: a window restored onto a monitor that has gone would otherwise
    open jammed into the nearest corner. The test is half-open, as `NativeRect` is, so a rectangle
    merely touching the edge counts as off-screen.
  - `HeightForContent(currentHeight, contentHeight, viewportHeight, minimumHeight)` — a measured
    content height turned into a window height by the difference between what the window is and
    what it shows today, so the title bar and the scroller's padding are carried rather than added
    up by hand. May shrink as well as grow, down to the floor. Unit-agnostic, and all four values
    must share a unit.
  - `ContentFittedHeight(…)` with `HeightCap` and `DefaultHeightFraction` — the popup case: content
    at the window's scale plus its frame, floored at a minimum and capped at a share of the work
    area, four fifths by default, past which the scroller takes over. A negative chrome reading is
    ignored rather than subtracted.
  - `ToPhysicalPixels(units, scale)` — device-independent units at a scale, rounded up so a pixel
    short does not clip the last row, and treating anything at or below zero as 100 % rather than
    collapsing the window.

## Which bound wins

`ContentFittedHeight` takes a floor and a fraction of the work area, and they can disagree. **The
floor wins.** A window shorter than its minimum has no room for what it must always show, while one
taller than the fraction is only taller than the policy prefers. The work area itself is the single
bound nothing passes, so a floor larger than the monitor gives a window the size of the work area
and no more.

## What a consumer on 0.9.0 gets

Nothing it had changes. `WindowFit` is additive, calls nothing, and holds no state; the placement
arithmetic an application wrote for itself can be deleted in favour of it at whatever pace suits.

`ZeroZero.Controls.WinUI` 0.8.0 drives it from a WinUI window in `PopupWindowFit`, for an
application that wants the popup case wired rather than assembled.
