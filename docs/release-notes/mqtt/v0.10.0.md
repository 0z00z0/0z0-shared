**Certificate trust has two modes, and a connection failure after an accepted certificate reports
what actually happened.**

**Released** 2026-09-29

**Minor, and it breaks.** `MqttCertificateTrustMode.Thumbprint` and
`MqttCertificateTrustMode.Certificate` are removed, with everything that served them.

## Two certificate-trust modes

`MqttCertificateTrustMode` is `System` or `AcceptAny`. The platform's own trust already covers a
genuine certificate, and accepting any certificate is the one deliberate override for a broker whose
certificate cannot be made to verify. Pinning a thumbprint or a certificate added a choice nobody
needed, and one that had to be kept working correctly.

Removed from the public surface:

- `MqttCertificateTrust`: `Thumbprint`, `Certificate`, `ForThumbprint`, both `ForCertificate`
  overloads, and `Validate()`.
- `MqttPresentedCertificate`: `Thumbprint` and `RawData`; it carries `SystemTrusted` alone.
- `MqttBrokerEdits`: `TrustValue`, `ValidateTrust()` and `SelectTrust(...)`. `TrustMode` is set
  directly.
- The settings panel's pinned-value box under **Certificate trust**, and the strings
  `TrustThumbprint`, `TrustCertificate`, `TrustNotAThumbprint`, `TrustNotACertificate`,
  `PlaceholderThumbprint` and `PlaceholderCertificate`.

A host using either removed mode switches to `MqttCertificateTrust.SystemTrust` where the broker's
certificate chains to a trusted root, and to `MqttCertificateTrust.AcceptAny` where it cannot.

## A failure after an accepted certificate is not reported as a trust failure

A connection whose certificate was accepted and which then failed for another reason — a proxy
answering the WebSocket upgrade with an HTTP status such as 530 — was reported as the broker's
certificate not being trusted. It now shows the real failure: the exception's type and message,
classified as `Failed`. The handshake records whether the trust setting accepted or refused the
certificate, and only a refused one is `TlsUntrusted`.

The clear-text retry stays blocked after an accepted certificate, as it does after a refused one:
encryption was on offer either way. A broker with no TLS on its port is still `TlsUnsupported` and
still reached in clear text under Automatic.

`MqttProbe.ClassifyConnectException` takes a `MqttCertificateVerdict?` (`NotPresented`, `Accepted`,
`Rejected`) in place of `bool? certificatePresented`. Wording an application logs or displays for a
failed connection may change, since a failure that used to read as an untrusted certificate now
reads as what happened.

## Unchanged

The endpoint search, the other probe outcomes, and `ZeroZero.Mqtt.Discovery`.
